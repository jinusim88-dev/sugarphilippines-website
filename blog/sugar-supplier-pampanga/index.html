Pampanga — The Culinary Capital's Sugar Needs

Pampanga has a unique position in the Philippine food landscape:

Kapampangan bakeries and sweets

The province is famous for pastillas, tibuk-tibuk, sans rival, ensaymada, and turrones de casuy — all of which depend on sugar. Bakeries and sweets producers in San Fernando, Angeles, Guagua, and Apalit consume significant volumes of washed and refined sugar.

Restaurant industry

Pampanga's culinary reputation drives a massive restaurant scene, from iconic sisig spots to fine dining. Commissaries and central kitchens serving these restaurants need scheduled sugar supply.

Clark Freeport Zone

Clark hosts international food companies, hotels, casinos, and duty-free retailers. Many of these operations require certified refined sugar (often ICUMSA 45 with Halal certification) with proper quality documentation.

Food manufacturing and snack production

Industrial zones in Mabalacat, Porac, and around Clark host food manufacturers producing snacks, beverages, and processed foods at scale.

Kakanin and rice cake heritage

Apalit, Macabebe, and the southern Pampanga river delta have a strong tradition of producing bibingka, puto, kutsinta, and other rice-based sweets — products that work well with raw or washed sugar.

Export-oriented producers

Several Pampanga-based food manufacturers produce for export markets, requiring ICUMSA 45 sugar with Halal, FSSC, and other certifications.
